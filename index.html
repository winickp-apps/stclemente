the story of

ST. CLEMENTE

History has always belonged, in part, to those born with an extraordinary sense of curiosity.

Artists, adventurers, writers and wonderfully restless souls who longed to explore, to discover and to experience a world beyond the one they knew. Those who found the familiar never quite enough.

Drawn to distant shores and unfamiliar cultures, they travelled, fell in love, collected stories and created beautiful things. Some returned home with extraordinary tales. Others were rather less fortunate.

For curiosity, as history so charmingly reminds us, has a peculiar habit of leading even the most well-intentioned souls into a rather extraordinary spot of trouble.

Among them were British wanderers who found themselves on the wrong side of foreign laws, far from the familiarity of home and often time behind prison walls. Their stories survive in handwritten appeals for mercy and letters of clemency, preserving their hopes, fears and longing for freedom across the ages.

Centuries later, I found myself unexpectedly among their company.

A Welshwoman by birth, and a wanderer by nature, I had spent the better part of a decade making a home beneath foreign skies. Yet, in a tumultuous twist of fate, I found myself behind the walls of a Middle Eastern prison, accused of a crime I had not committed.

In a land I had come to know and love, I was suddenly at the mercy of laws I scarcely understood, and had never felt quite so far from the rolling hills of Wales.

An experience that would change me profoundly.

Some might have thought such an experience would put an end to my wandering spirit. Quite the contrary.

For it seems curiosity, despite its rather questionable track record, is a difficult habit to break.

Naturally, I began to wonder about the others who had found themselves in similarly unfortunate predicaments. Who were these curious souls? What peculiar twists of fate had landed them in such trouble?

I began searching through the archives, following the stories of wanderers, the accused and the misunderstood, until I came upon the most extraordinary collection of handwritten appeals.

Letters pleading for mercy, filled with hope, fear and longing. Beautifully composed words, ink fading into parchment. Each a little piece of someone’s story, preserved long after its author had disappeared.

I found something deeply moving in the contrast. Such beautiful things, born of such difficult circumstances. And among those beautiful old letters, one word resonated more deeply than the rest.

Clemency.

The granting of mercy, compassion and the possibility of a second chance. A word that had become rather close to my own heart.

And somewhere between their search for clemency and my own, Saint Clemente was born.

An alter ego, perhaps. A kindred spirit to those curious wanderers who had come before me. Born from their letters of mercy and a lifelong love of writing and painting, Saint Clemente became my way of turning a rather unfortunate chapter into something beautiful.

With ink and time-worn fabric, I create pieces that borrow their romance from another age. Inspired by faded letters of clemency, my British heritage and my travels to faraway places, each work carries a whisper of the past, whilst telling a story that is entirely my own.

A little romantic, a little rebellious, and rather unwilling to let misfortune have the final word.

“Every saint has a past,
and every sinner has a future.”

— Oscar Wilde
